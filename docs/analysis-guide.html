Stock Technical Analysis Guide
An interactive walkthrough of every indicator used in the Stock Analysis API. Each section explains the formula, shows a live chart, and tells you when it generates a BUY or SELL signal.
What is Technical Analysis?
  • → Studies past price and volume to forecast future movement — ignores fundamentals like earnings or P/E ratio.
  • → No single indicator is reliable alone. This API combines 7 indicators into a weighted scoring system to reduce false signals.
  • → Two families of signals: trend-following (SMA, EMA, MACD) ride momentum, mean-reversion (RSI, Bollinger, Stochastic) catch extremes.
  • → Always treat recommendations as one input — not a guaranteed prediction.

SMA — Simple Moving Average
The simplest trend indicator. It averages the closing price over a rolling window, smoothing out daily noise so the underlying trend becomes visible.
Formula
SMA20 = (Close₁ + Close₂ + … + Close₂₀) / 20
SMA50 = (Close₁ + Close₂ + … + Close₅₀) / 50

Every new day: add today's close, drop the oldest — the window slides forward.
Chart — Price with SMA20 (amber) & SMA50 (purple)
Signals & Notes
  • →BUY   SMA20 crosses above SMA50 — "Golden Cross". Short-term average is rising faster than long-term.
  • →SELL   SMA20 crosses below SMA50 — "Death Cross". Short-term trend falling through long-term.
  • →LAG   SMA is the most lagging indicator here — it confirms a trend after it has already started. Weight: 0.5 (lowest).
  • →SMA50 requires 50 days of data. The API defaults to a 90-day period to ensure SMA50 is always available.
EMA — Exponential Moving Average
Like SMA, but recent prices carry more weight. This makes EMA react faster to price changes — catching trend shifts earlier but with slightly more false signals.
Formula
multiplier = 2 / (span + 1)
EMA_today = EMA_yesterday + multiplier × (Close − EMA_yesterday)

EMA20 multiplier = 2/21 ≈ 0.095 (reacts quickly)
EMA50 multiplier = 2/51 ≈ 0.038 (reacts slowly)
Chart — EMA20 (green) vs EMA50 (red) with Price
SMA vs EMA Comparison
  • →EMA crossovers happen earlier than SMA crossovers — less lag, more responsive to recent moves.
  • →EMA crossover is the primary trend signal in this API (weight 1.0); SMA crossover is a slower confirmation (weight 0.5).
  • →BUY   EMA20 > EMA50 — uptrend confirmed by fast average
  • →SELL   EMA20 < EMA50 — downtrend confirmed by fast average
MACD — Moving Average Convergence Divergence
MACD captures the momentum behind a trend — how fast it is accelerating or decelerating. It's one of the most widely trusted indicators in technical analysis.
Formula
MACD Line = EMA(12) − EMA(26) ← difference of two EMAs
Signal Line = EMA(9) of MACD Line ← smoothed version of MACD
Histogram = MACD Line − Signal Line ← visual divergence

MACD > 0 → EMA12 above EMA26 → uptrend territory
MACD < 0 → EMA12 below EMA26 → downtrend territory
Chart — MACD Line (blue), Signal (amber), Histogram (green/red bars)
Signals
  • →BUY   MACD Line crosses above Signal Line — bullish momentum is picking up.
  • →SELL   MACD Line crosses below Signal Line — bearish momentum is picking up.
  • →Histogram bars show the gap — growing green bars = strengthening uptrend; growing red bars = strengthening downtrend.
  • →Weight: 2.0 (highest) — captures real momentum shifts, not just price levels.
RSI — Relative Strength Index
RSI measures the speed and size of recent price changes to identify overbought or oversold conditions. It oscillates between 0 and 100.
Formula
Average Gain = mean of all up-days over last 14 days
Average Loss = mean of all down-days over last 14 days
RS = Average Gain / Average Loss
RSI = 100 − (100 / (1 + RS))

RSI → 100 price only went up → extremely overbought
RSI → 0 price only went down → extremely oversold
Chart — RSI with Overbought (70) & Oversold (30) zones
Signals
  • →BUY   RSI < 30 — oversold. Price has fallen too far, too fast. Statistically likely to bounce.
  • →SELL   RSI > 70 — overbought. Price has risen too far, too fast. Correction likely.
  • →CAUTION   In a strong downtrend, RSI can stay below 30 for weeks. Always pair with trend indicators like MACD or ADX.
  • →Weight: 1.5 — proven mean-reversion signal but context-dependent.
Bollinger Bands
Bollinger Bands form a dynamic envelope around price based on current volatility. Wide bands = volatile market. Narrow bands ("squeeze") = a big move is likely approaching.
Formula
Middle Band = SMA(20)
Upper Band = SMA(20) + 2 × StdDev(20-day close)
Lower Band = SMA(20) − 2 × StdDev(20-day close)

By the 2-sigma rule, ~95% of price action falls inside the bands.
Touching a band = statistically unusual — mean reversion expected.
Chart — Price (blue) with Upper (red) / Middle / Lower (green) Bands
Signals
  • →BUY   Price touches or breaks below the Lower Band — statistically extreme low, bounce likely.
  • →SELL   Price touches or breaks above the Upper Band — statistically extreme high, pullback likely.
  • →Bands automatically widen during high volatility and narrow during calm — they adapt to current conditions unlike fixed SMA levels.
  • →Weight: 2.0 (highest) — combines price extremes with volatility context.
Stochastic Oscillator
Stochastic tells you where the current closing price sits within the recent high-low range. Is the stock closing near the top or bottom of its recent range?
Formula
%K = (Close − Lowest Low₁₄) / (Highest High₁₄ − Lowest Low₁₄) × 100
%D = 3-period SMA of %K ← the signal line

%K = 100 → closing at the highest point in 14 days (top of range)
%K = 0 → closing at the lowest point in 14 days (bottom of range)
Chart — %K (blue) & %D Signal (amber) with zones at 20 & 80
Signals
  • →BUY   %K < 20 — price near the bottom of its recent range (oversold).
  • →SELL   %K > 80 — price near the top of its recent range (overbought).
  • →Best signal: %K crosses %D from below while both are in the oversold zone (or vice versa for sell).
  • →Complements RSI — both measure mean reversion but via entirely different maths. When both agree, confidence is higher. Weight: 1.5.
OBV — On Balance Volume
OBV tracks whether volume is flowing into or out of a stock. A price rise backed by high volume is more trustworthy than one on low volume.
Formula
If Close > Previous Close: OBV = OBV_prev + Volume (buyers dominate)
If Close < Previous Close: OBV = OBV_prev − Volume (sellers dominate)
If Close = Previous Close: OBV = OBV_prev (no change)

Rising OBV = buying pressure accumulating (bullish)
Falling OBV = selling pressure accumulating (bearish)
Chart — OBV (blue) vs OBV EMA20 (amber dashed)
Signals
  • →BUY   OBV above its EMA20 — volume is trending bullish, buyers are accumulating.
  • →SELL   OBV below its EMA20 — volume is trending bearish, sellers are distributing.
  • →Divergence is the most powerful OBV signal: price makes a new high but OBV doesn't → the rally is weakening (distribution in disguise).
  • →Weight: 1.0 — volume confirms price moves made by the "smart money".
ATR — Average True Range
ATR measures volatility — how much a stock moves per day on average. It does not indicate direction; it tells you how much risk to expect on any given trade.
Formula
True Range = max(
  High − Low,
  |High − Previous Close|,
  |Low − Previous Close|
)
ATR14 = 14-period rolling average of True Range

True Range uses previous close to capture overnight gaps.
Chart — ATR14 over time (orange = volatility level)
How to Use ATR
  • →ATR is a risk measurement tool, not a BUY/SELL signal. It is included in the response for reference.
  • →High ATR → large daily swings → more risk and more potential reward per day.
  • →Low ATR → calm price action → consolidation phase, often before a breakout.
  • →Practical use: set stop-loss at Entry − 1.5 × ATR14 to avoid being stopped out by normal noise.
ADX — Average Directional Index
ADX answers one question: how strong is the current trend? — not which direction. It acts as a confidence filter: weak ADX reduces the score because sideways markets produce false signals.
Formula
+DM = today's High − yesterday's High (if positive, else 0)
−DM = yesterday's Low − today's Low (if positive, else 0)
+DI = 100 × EMA(+DM, 14) / ATR14
−DI = 100 × EMA(−DM, 14) / ATR14
DX = |+DI − −DI| / (+DI + −DI) × 100
ADX = EMA(DX, 14) ← always 0–100, regardless of direction
Chart — ADX with Trending (20) & Strong (40) thresholds
Interpretation
  • →ADX < 20 — No clear trend. Ranging/sideways market. All BUY/SELL signals are less reliable. Confidence reduced by 25%.
  • →ADX 20–40 — Moderate trend. Normal confidence. Most indicators work well here.
  • →ADX > 40 — Strong trend. High confidence. Trend-following signals are very reliable.
  • →Key insight: ADX rises for both uptrends AND downtrends. A stock in a strong downtrend can have ADX = 60.
Scoring & Recommendation System
Each indicator casts a weighted vote for BUY or SELL. The winning side's score versus the maximum possible gives a confidence percentage.
Weight Table — Total possible = 9.5 pts
IndicatorWeightReason for weight
MACD crossover 2.0
 Captures trend momentum acceleration — strong predictor
Bollinger Bands 2.0
 Adapts to volatility — price extremes are statistically grounded
RSI 1.5
 Proven mean-reversion signal across decades of use
Stochastic 1.5
 Confirms RSI via a different calculation approach
EMA crossover 1.0
 Faster trend direction signal than SMA
OBV trend 1.0
 Volume confirms price — reveals smart-money accumulation
SMA crossover 0.5
 Most lagging indicator — slow confirmation role only
Confidence Calculation
confidence = (winning_score / 9.5) × 100

If ADX < 20 → confidence = confidence × 0.75 (sideways market penalty)

Key Takeaways
  • →A BUY signal means the balance of evidence leans bullish — not a guaranteed outcome.
  • →Confidence < 40% always overrides to HOLD — conflicting signals are not worth acting on.
  • →ADX < 20 reduces confidence by 25% — ranging markets generate many false signals. Wait for a trend.
  • →When MACD and Bollinger Bands both agree (combined weight 4.0), that alone can push confidence past 40%.
  • →Always consider ATR before sizing a position — a high ATR means larger stop-loss is needed to survive normal volatility.
